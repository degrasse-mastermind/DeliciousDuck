Subject: A quick thermometer check before duck night
Preview: Check the tool you already own before buying another.

Before your next duck dinner, take a minute to check your thermometer.

Follow its manufacturer's instructions for an ice-water accuracy check, make sure the battery works, and clean the probe before and after use. Insert it from the side into the thickest part of a breast, keeping the sensing area in the meat and away from the pan.

USDA recommends a measured minimum internal temperature of 165°F (74°C) for duck, including breasts. Don't rely on color or an assumed rise while the meat rests.

Subject: Your small-table Thanksgiving equipment check
Preview: Pan, rack, fridge space—and a thermometer that works.

A small Thanksgiving table still benefits from a little planning before the bird arrives.

Check that your pan and rack fit the oven, that the thawing bird has room in the refrigerator, and that your thermometer has a working battery. Use equipment you already own when it does the job.

An instant-read thermometer is useful for final spot checks. A wired leave-in probe can help you follow a whole roast, but one probe only reads one location. Keep the display outside the oven and follow the manufacturer's probe and cable limits.

USDA recommends cooking a whole duck to a measured minimum of 165°F (74°C). Check the innermost thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast, avoiding bone. Color and expected carryover aren't substitutes for those readings.
