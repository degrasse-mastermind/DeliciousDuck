# DeliciousDuck Illustration Art Direction

## Role

Illustration is a deliberate DeliciousDuck editorial instrument, not a default decoration and not a substitute for missing photography. Use it when explanation, anatomy, abstraction, sequence, or brand character becomes clearer than it would in a photograph. Use photography whenever appetite, surface texture, browning, rendered fat, finished-dish proof, or cooking credibility is the visual promise.

## Standard

The target is premium culinary editorial illustration: controlled enough for a serious food publication, warm enough to remain approachable, and precise enough to support technical instruction.

- Confident, economical contours with deliberate line-weight hierarchy.
- Accurate duck-breast, fat-cap, muscle, knife, probe, pan and cookware anatomy.
- Strong, readable silhouette at thumbnail size.
- One focal idea per frame with generous negative space for typography.
- Restricted brand palette: forest ink, warm ivory, golden duck, restrained cranberry and neutral food tones.
- Texture used sparingly to describe material—not to fill empty space.
- Consistent perspective, light direction, scale and visual grammar across still and motion crops.
- Sophisticated editorial finish; never cute, whimsical, crafty or pseudo-handmade.
- Diagram labels and exact copy remain deterministic overlays outside the illustration.

## Line and surface language

Use a clean ink or vector-like contour with subtle analogue character: smooth primary silhouette, finer secondary anatomy lines, and selective hatch or stipple only where form would otherwise be ambiguous. The drawing must survive animation, masking and parallax without revealing noisy edges. Avoid loose pencil shading, muddy washes, fuzzy watercolor blooms, airbrushed gradients and inconsistent contour density.

## Composition

- Isolate the subject on transparent or true warm-ivory ground.
- Reserve at least 38–45% of the frame as calm text space where the brief requests it.
- Keep important anatomy inside a centre-safe 4:5 crop and define 9:16 extensions when motion use is required.
- No decorative herbs, table scatter, napkins or cookware unless they explain the lesson.
- Never use an oversized duck mascot as a substitute for the food or technique.

## Accuracy and review

Every technical illustration requires review against a real photographic/anatomical reference before approval. Reject inconsistent perspective, probe paths through the fat cap, scoring cuts into meat, physically impossible pan contact, ambiguous fat pooling, or cookware that changes shape between frames.

## Delivery contract

Approved library art should be delivered as layered SVG where practical plus a 2400px PNG master with alpha or true warm-ivory ground. Motion-capable art separates subject, pan/tool, annotation path and optional shadow into named layers. The slot registry—not template code—owns the active file path and approval state.

## Production source policy

Production illustration must be supplied and explicitly approved as external source artwork. Codex-drawn SVG artwork is not an acceptable production source. Creative Studio may render, crop, composite and animate approved artwork, but it must not silently generate or substitute an illustration when an approval-required slot is empty.

Until approved artwork is installed, a technical slot remains `approval-required`. Internal preview mode may show the Studio-owned neutral placeholder, clearly labelled as such; it must never be represented as final creative. Factual typography, labels, temperatures, measurements, arrows, callouts, CTA copy and branding remain deterministic Studio overlays rather than baked illustration content.
